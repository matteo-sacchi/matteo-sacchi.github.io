Research

Understanding QFT beyond perturbation theory

One of the key challenges in modern theoretical physics is understanding quantum field theories (QFTs) beyond their perturbative regime. My research focuses on non-perturbative aspects of QFTs with additional symmetries, like conformal and supersymmetry, which provide computational control while still exhibiting rich physics.

Currently, I am working on these topics as a Research Assistant Professor at the Simons Center for Geometry and Physics, after a postdoc at the University of Oxford. Previously, I earned my Ph.D. from Università degli Studi di Milano-Bicocca in 2021, with a thesis on dualities and symmetry enhancements in 3- and 4-dimensional QFTs.

A large part of my research has been devoted to dualities and correspondences in QFTs, such as infrared dualities, mirror symmetry and gauge/CFT correspondences. These provide equivalent descriptions of the same physical system, which are often more tractable and thus represent a powerful tool for investigating its properties. When it comes to supersymmetric and conformal QFTs, various quantities can be expressed exactly in terms of special functions that also appear in the mathematical literature. This fosters a fruitful connection which enhances our understanding of both QFTs and mathematics.

Currently, I am developing a 2d/2d correspondence that relates the sphere partition functions of certain 2d 𝒩 = (2,2) unitary superconformal field theories (SCFTs) and the correlation functions of non-unitary 2d conformal field theories (CFTs) on a Riemann surface [Rastelli–Rayhaun–Sacchi–Zafrir, 2026]. This can be derived by considering a parent 4d 𝒩 = 2 SCFT on S2 × Σ: reduction on Σ leads to the 2d 𝒩 = (2,2) theory on S2, while reduction on S2 yields the non-unitary 2d CFT on Σ. The correspondence upgrades the chiral algebra associated to the 4d 𝒩 = 2 SCFT by the SCFT/VOA correspondence [Beem et al., 2015] to a full-fledged 2d CFT. Moreover, it can be viewed as a “2 + 2 = 4” analog of the “4 + 2 = 6” AGT correspondence [Alday–Gaiotto–Tachikawa, 2010].

I have also been interested in generalized symmetries of QFTs. Symmetries are extremely powerful to constrain the dynamics of a physics system, for example via anomalies and selection rules. The new generalizations of the usual notion of symmetry have led to tremendous advancements in our understanding of QFTs. My work aims to uncover how these generalized symmetries relate to partition functions in supersymmetric QFTs.
